10 Best Places to See Cherry Blossoms in Hangzhou

  • Home
  • Travel Blog
  • Blog Details

When spring arrives in Hangzhou, the city transforms into a dreamscape of pink and white petals. Cherry blossoms, or yinghua as they are called in Chinese, are not just flowers here—they are a cultural phenomenon. Every year, locals and tourists alike flock to parks, lakesides, and temple grounds to witness the fleeting beauty of sakura. If you are planning a trip to Hangzhou during the cherry blossom season, which typically peaks from mid-March to early April, you are in for a treat. This guide will take you through the 10 best places to see cherry blossoms in Hangzhou, each offering a unique blend of nature, history, and local charm. Let’s dive in.

1. West Lake: The Iconic Cherry Blossom Backdrop

No list of cherry blossom spots in Hangzhou would be complete without mentioning West Lake. This UNESCO World Heritage site is the heart of the city, and during spring, its shores are lined with thousands of cherry trees. The most famous stretch is along Bai Causeway and Su Causeway, where the blossoms create a tunnel of pink that perfectly frames the lake’s pagodas and bridges.

Why It’s a Must-Visit

  • Photography Paradise: The combination of cherry blossoms, willow trees, and the lake’s reflective surface makes for stunning photos, especially during sunrise or sunset.
  • Cultural Immersion: You can rent a traditional Chinese boat and drift among the petals, or simply walk the causeway while locals practice tai chi under the trees.
  • Accessibility: West Lake is centrally located and easily reachable by bus, taxi, or even bike. The cherry blossom area is free to enter, though boat rentals cost extra.

Pro Tip

Arrive early in the morning (around 6:30 AM) to avoid the crowds. By 9 AM, the area becomes packed with selfie sticks and tour groups. Also, keep an eye on the weather—a sudden rain shower can strip the blossoms in hours.

2. Taiziwan Park: The Cherry Blossom Garden

If you want a dedicated cherry blossom experience, Taiziwan Park is your best bet. Located near West Lake, this park was originally a royal garden during the Southern Song Dynasty. Today, it boasts over 2,000 cherry trees, including rare varieties like the Somei Yoshino and Yamazakura.

What Makes It Special

  • Blossom Diversity: Unlike other spots, Taiziwan has early, mid, and late-blooming varieties, extending the viewing season from March to early April.
  • Theme Zones: The park is divided into sections like the “Snowy Fragrance Path” and “Petal Rain Alley,” each designed to maximize your sensory experience.
  • Family-Friendly: There are wide lawns, picnic areas, and a small pond where children can feed koi fish. It’s a great spot for a relaxing afternoon.

Logistics

  • Admission: Free
  • Best Time: Late March, when the Somei Yoshino trees are at their peak.
  • Getting There: Take bus routes 4, 7, or 15 to the Taiziwan stop. Alternatively, walk from West Lake’s south shore—it’s about a 15-minute stroll.

3. Hangzhou Botanical Garden: A Scientific Wonderland

For those who appreciate cherry blossoms beyond their beauty, the Hangzhou Botanical Garden offers an educational twist. Established in 1965, this garden is home to a dedicated Sakura collection, featuring over 30 species of cherry trees from Japan, China, and Korea.

Highlights

  • Rare Species: You can see the Shidarezakura (weeping cherry) and Kanzan (double-flowered cherry), which are less common in other parks.
  • Greenhouse Bonus: The garden also has a tropical greenhouse, where you can escape the spring chill and see orchids and cacti.
  • Quiet Atmosphere: Unlike the bustling West Lake, the botanical garden is a haven for solitude seekers. You can easily spend two hours wandering the cherry tree lanes without bumping into a crowd.

Practical Info

  • Address: No. 1 Taoyuanling, Xihu District
  • Ticket Price: 10 RMB (about $1.40)
  • Opening Hours: 7:00 AM to 5:30 PM

4. Lingyin Temple: Cherry Blossoms Meets Spirituality

If you want to combine cherry blossom viewing with a touch of Zen, head to Lingyin Temple. This ancient Buddhist temple, which dates back to 326 AD, is surrounded by lush hills and, in spring, a sea of cherry blossoms. The contrast between the temple’s yellow walls and the pink petals is nothing short of magical.

The Experience

  • Spiritual Vibe: The sound of monks chanting and the scent of incense mix with the floral fragrance, creating a meditative atmosphere.
  • Scenic Hikes: Behind the temple, there are trails that lead up to the hills, where you can see cherry blossoms growing wild among bamboo groves.
  • Cultural Events: During the blossom season, the temple sometimes hosts tea ceremonies or calligraphy workshops. Check their official WeChat account for schedules.

Tips for Visitors

  • Dress Modestly: Since it’s an active religious site, avoid wearing shorts or sleeveless tops.
  • Don’t Miss the Feilai Feng Caves: These ancient Buddhist carvings are located just outside the temple and are covered in moss and cherry petals—a photographer’s dream.

5. Longjing Village: Cherry Blossoms in Tea Country

Hangzhou is famous for its Longjing tea, and Longjing Village is where the magic happens. During spring, the village’s terraced tea fields are dotted with cherry trees, creating a stunning green-and-pink patchwork. This is a less touristy spot, perfect for those who want to escape the city noise.

Why You Should Go

  • Unique Landscape: Instead of manicured parks, you get rolling hills covered in tea bushes and wild cherry trees. The view is raw and natural.
  • Tea Tasting: Most tea houses in the village offer free samples of Longjing tea. Sit on a terrace overlooking the blossoms and sip the freshest green tea of your life.
  • Bike-Friendly: You can rent a bicycle from the village entrance and ride through the tea fields. The air is crisp, and the roads are quiet.

Getting There

Take bus 27 from the city center to the Longjing Village stop. Alternatively, a taxi from West Lake costs about 30 RMB. The village is small, so you can explore it on foot in two hours.

6. Prince Bay Park (Gongwang Bay): A Hidden Gem

Located near the Qiantang River, Prince Bay Park is a relatively new park that opened in 2018. It has quickly become a favorite among locals for its well-maintained cherry blossom groves and modern facilities. The park is named after a historical figure, Prince Qian, who once owned a villa here.

What to Expect

  • Blossom Tunnel: The park’s main path is lined with cherry trees that form a natural archway. When the wind blows, petals rain down like confetti.
  • River Views: The park sits on the banks of the Qiantang River, so you can combine cherry blossom viewing with a riverside walk. On clear days, you can see the distant mountains.
  • Photography Spots: There are several wooden bridges and pagodas that provide excellent framing for your photos.

Visitor Info

  • Admission: Free
  • Best Time: Late March to early April
  • Nearby Attractions: The park is close to the Hangzhou Olympic Sports Center, so you can also catch a sports event if you’re lucky.

7. Zhejiang University (Zijingang Campus): Youth and Blossoms

For a dose of youthful energy, visit the Zijingang Campus of Zhejiang University. This modern campus is known for its cherry blossom avenue, which runs from the main gate to the library. During spring, students picnic under the trees, play guitar, or simply study in the open air.

Why It’s Worth It

  • Academic Vibe: The campus has a lively, intellectual atmosphere. You might even stumble upon a student art exhibition or a poetry reading related to cherry blossoms.
  • Budget-Friendly: There’s no entrance fee, and the campus has affordable cafeterias where you can grab a meal for under 20 RMB.
  • Instagram-Worthy: The contrast between the modern architecture and the soft pink blossoms is visually striking. The library building, with its glass facade, reflects the cherry trees beautifully.

How to Get There

Take Metro Line 2 to the Zijingang Station, then walk 10 minutes to the campus. The cherry blossom avenue is near the east gate.

8. Hangzhou Flower Harbor (Huagang Guan Yu): Fish and Flowers

Huagang Guan Yu, or Flower Harbor, is a classic West Lake scenic spot that is famous for its red koi fish and, of course, cherry blossoms. The park is designed in a traditional Chinese garden style, with winding paths, pavilions, and a large pond.

The Attractions

  • Koi Pond: You can buy fish food (5 RMB per bag) and feed the colorful koi while cherry blossoms float on the water’s surface. It’s a serene and interactive experience.
  • Pavilion Views: The park’s central pavilion offers a panoramic view of the cherry trees and the lake. It’s a popular spot for wedding photos.
  • Seasonal Events: During the blossom peak, the park sometimes hosts a “Cherry Blossom Festival” with folk music performances and lantern displays.

Practical Tips

  • Best Time: Late March
  • Crowds: This is a popular spot, so visit on a weekday if possible.
  • Nearby Food: There are several street food stalls selling tanghulu (candied hawthorn) and baozi (steamed buns). Try the cherry blossom-flavored ice cream—it’s a seasonal specialty.

9. Xianghu Lake: A Suburban Escape

If you want to avoid the crowds of West Lake, drive 30 minutes south to Xianghu Lake. This man-made lake is surrounded by parks and gardens, including a dedicated cherry blossom forest with over 10,000 trees. Yes, you read that right—10,000.

What Makes It Unique

  • Scale: The sheer number of trees creates a “sea of pink” effect that is overwhelming in the best way possible.
  • Activities: You can rent a paddleboat, ride a miniature train through the forest, or even camp overnight (camping gear rentals available).
  • Night Viewing: Some areas of the park are illuminated at night, allowing for yozakura (night cherry blossom viewing). The blossoms look ethereal under soft lights.

Logistics

  • Admission: 60 RMB for adults
  • Transportation: Take bus 327 from the city center to Xianghu Station, or drive via the G92 highway. Parking is available for 10 RMB per hour.
  • Best Time: Early April, when the late-blooming varieties are at their peak.

10. Jinghang Grand Canal: Blossoms on the Water

The Jinghang Grand Canal, a UNESCO World Heritage site, runs through Hangzhou and offers a unique cherry blossom experience. The canal’s banks are lined with cherry trees, especially near the Gongchen Bridge and Xixing Ferry Terminal. You can walk, bike, or take a canal cruise to enjoy the blossoms.

Why It’s Different

  • Historical Context: The canal was built over 2,500 years ago, and walking along its banks feels like stepping back in time. The cherry blossoms add a layer of romance to the ancient waterway.
  • Boat Cruises: Several companies offer cherry blossom-themed cruises, where you can sip tea and watch the petals drift by. Prices start at 50 RMB per person.
  • Local Life: This area is less touristy than West Lake, so you’ll see locals fishing, practicing calligraphy, or playing mahjong under the cherry trees.

Tips for Exploring

  • Best Section: Start at the Gongchen Bridge and walk north for about 2 kilometers. The trees are densest near the bridge.
  • Timing: Late March is ideal, but early April works too.
  • Food Stops: There are several small restaurants along the canal serving heye baozhu (lotus leaf rice) and dongpo pork. Try the cherry blossom tea—it’s a local specialty.

When to Go: Timing Your Cherry Blossom Trip

Cherry blossoms in Hangzhou are notoriously fickle. The peak season usually falls between March 20 and April 5, but this can shift by a week depending on the weather. Here’s a quick breakdown:

  • Early Bloomers (March 10-20): Kawazu-zakura and Yamazakura varieties at Taiziwan Park and the Botanical Garden.
  • Mid Bloomers (March 20-30): Somei Yoshino at West Lake, Prince Bay Park, and Zhejiang University.
  • Late Bloomers (April 1-10): Kanzan and Shidarezakura at Xianghu Lake and Longjing Village.

Check the Hangzhou Cherry Blossom Forecast online before you go. Many Chinese weather apps, like Moji or Weather China, provide daily updates on blossom progress.

How to Get Around: Transportation Tips

Hangzhou has an excellent public transportation system, but cherry blossom season can be chaotic. Here are some practical tips:

  • Metro: Lines 1, 2, and 4 cover most of the spots mentioned. Buy a temporary metro card at any station—it costs 20 RMB (deposit included).
  • Bike Sharing: Apps like Mobike and Hellobike are everywhere. Renting a bike for a day costs about 10 RMB.
  • Taxis: Didi (China’s Uber) is reliable. A ride from West Lake to Xianghu Lake costs around 50 RMB.
  • Walking: Many spots are within walking distance of each other. For example, Taiziwan Park is a 15-minute walk from West Lake’s south shore.

What to Pack for Cherry Blossom Viewing

Spring in Hangzhou can be unpredictable—sunny one moment, drizzly the next. Pack smartly:

  • Comfortable Shoes: You’ll be walking a lot. Sneakers or flat sandals are best.
  • Light Jacket: Temperatures range from 10°C to 20°C (50°F to 68°F). A windbreaker or denim jacket works well.
  • Umbrella or Raincoat: Sudden showers are common. A compact umbrella is handy.
  • Camera or Smartphone: You’ll regret not having one. A polarizing filter helps reduce glare from the lake.
  • Snacks and Water: Some parks have limited food options. Bring a bottle of water and some mooncakes or crackers.

Local Etiquette: Do’s and Don’ts

Hangzhou locals take their cherry blossoms seriously. Follow these rules to avoid dirty looks:

  • Do Not Pick the Flowers: It’s illegal and disrespectful. Enjoy them with your eyes, not your hands.
  • Do Not Step on the Grass: Many parks have designated paths. Stay on them to protect the tree roots.
  • Do Not Litter: Carry a small bag for your trash. Public bins are plentiful, but they fill up quickly during peak season.
  • Do Not Block Paths: If you’re taking photos, step to the side. The locals are patient, but nobody likes a photo hog.
  • Do Say “Xie Xie”: A simple “thank you” goes a long way, especially if you’re asking someone to take your photo.

Final Thoughts on Hangzhou’s Cherry Blossom Season

Hangzhou during cherry blossom season is a sensory overload in the best way possible. From the iconic West Lake to the hidden gem of Prince Bay Park, each spot offers a unique perspective on this fleeting natural wonder. Whether you’re a solo traveler seeking solitude in Longjing Village or a family looking for fun at Xianghu Lake, there’s a cherry blossom experience waiting for you. So pack your bags, book your flight, and get ready to be swept away by the pink petals of Hangzhou. Just remember: the blossoms wait for no one.
