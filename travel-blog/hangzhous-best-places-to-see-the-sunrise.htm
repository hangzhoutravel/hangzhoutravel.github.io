Hangzhou’s Best Places to See the Sunrise

  • Home
  • Travel Blog
  • Blog Details

There is a moment in Hangzhou when the sky turns from ink to lavender, when the mist lifts off West Lake like a silk veil, and the first rays of sunlight set the pagodas ablaze. It is a spectacle that has inspired poets, painters, and emperors for over a thousand years. But for the modern traveler, catching the sunrise in Hangzhou is not just about beauty—it is about beating the crowds, finding the perfect angle, and experiencing the city in its most serene, unvarnished state. Whether you are a hardcore photographer chasing golden hour, a couple seeking a romantic start to the day, or a solo traveler craving stillness, Hangzhou offers a variety of sunrise spots that cater to every mood and skill level.

In this guide, we will explore the best locations to watch the sun rise over Hangzhou, from the iconic lakefront to hidden hills and ancient temples. We will also dive into the practical details: when to go, what to bring, how to avoid the tourist buses, and which nearby cafes or breakfast stalls will reward your early wake-up call. Let the dawn begin.

Why Sunrise in Hangzhou Is a Must-Do

Hangzhou is one of China’s most visited cities, and for good reason. Its West Lake (Xihu) is a UNESCO World Heritage site, and its surrounding hills are dotted with tea plantations, bamboo groves, and Buddhist temples. But the city’s popularity means that by 9 a.m., the lakefront is already packed with selfie sticks, tour groups, and vendors. Sunrise, however, belongs to the early birds. At 5:30 a.m., the only sounds are birdsong, the gentle lapping of water, and the soft footsteps of tai chi practitioners. The light is soft, the air is cool, and the reflections on the lake are flawless.

Moreover, Hangzhou’s geography creates unique atmospheric conditions. The city is surrounded by low mountains and bisected by the Qiantang River, which often generates morning mist. This mist, combined with the first sunlight, produces a dreamlike, almost ethereal quality that is difficult to capture at any other time of day. For photographers, this is the “blue hour” on steroids. For everyone else, it is simply magic.

Top Sunrise Spots in Hangzhou

1. Baochu Pagoda (Baochu Ta) on the Hill

If you want a panoramic view of West Lake and the city skyline, head to the base of Baochu Pagoda. This slender, seven-story pagoda sits on a small hill just north of the lake. The hike up takes about 15 to 20 minutes from the nearest road, and the path is well-maintained with stone steps. It is not a difficult climb, but you will want to bring a flashlight or use your phone’s torch for the early morning ascent.

Why it is special: From the top, you get a bird’s-eye view of the entire lake, including the famous Broken Bridge (Duanqiao) and the island of Solitary Hill (Gushan). The pagoda itself is often silhouetted against the rising sun, making it a perfect subject for photos. On clear days, you can see the entire Hangzhou basin, with the Qiantang River glittering in the distance.

Best time to arrive: Aim to be at the top 30 minutes before official sunrise. The sky will begin to change color about 20 minutes before the sun appears, and that pre-dawn glow is often more beautiful than the actual sunrise.

Pro tip: Bring a wide-angle lens if you are a photographer. The combination of the pagoda, the lake, and the distant mountains creates a layered composition that is hard to beat. Also, be aware that the hill is popular with local morning exercisers, so you will have company, but it is a friendly, communal vibe.

2. The Broken Bridge (Duanqiao) at Dawn

The Broken Bridge is arguably the most famous spot on West Lake. It is the bridge where the legendary lovers Xu Xian and Bai Suzhen met in the classic Chinese folktale “The Legend of the White Snake.” During the day, it is a chaotic bottleneck of tourists. But at dawn, it transforms into a tranquil, almost sacred space.

Why it is special: The bridge’s name comes from a visual illusion: when snow covers the bridge, the reflection in the water makes it appear broken. But at sunrise, the bridge offers a perfect foreground for the rising sun over the lake. The eastern side of the lake is relatively open, so you get an unobstructed view of the horizon. The nearby willow trees and lotus ponds add texture to the scene.

Best time to arrive: 5:30 a.m. in summer, 6:00 a.m. in winter. The bridge itself is small, so you can easily find a spot to stand or sit. Bring a tripod if you want to capture long-exposure shots of the water.

Pro tip: After the sunrise, walk north along the lake toward the Gushan area. There is a small pavilion called Pinghu Qiuyue (Autumn Moon on Calm Lake) that offers a different perspective. Also, the nearby Starbucks on Beishan Road opens at 7 a.m., so you can grab a coffee while waiting for the crowds to arrive.

3. Leifeng Pagoda (Leifeng Ta) and the Southern Shore

Leifeng Pagoda is one of the most iconic structures in Hangzhou. Rebuilt in 2002 after the original collapsed in 1924, it now stands five stories tall on the southern shore of West Lake. While the pagoda itself is a popular tourist attraction during the day, the area around it is excellent for sunrise photography.

Why it is special: The pagoda is situated on a small hill, and the view from the base of the pagoda looks north across the lake toward the city skyline. The reflection of the pagoda in the water, combined with the morning mist, creates a classic Chinese painting effect. If you climb to the top of the pagoda (it opens at 8 a.m., so you cannot go up during sunrise), you can still get great shots from the ground level or from the nearby Nanping Hill.

Best time to arrive: 5:45 a.m. in summer. The area is less crowded than the northern shore, so you will have more space to set up your gear.

Pro tip: The best photo spot is not at the pagoda itself but at the small stone bridge just south of it, called Changqiao (Long Bridge). From there, you can frame the pagoda with the lake and the rising sun. Also, the nearby Jingci Temple opens its gates at 6 a.m., and the morning chanting of the monks is a beautiful audio backdrop.

4. The Nine Creeks and Eighteen Gullies (Jiuxi Shiba Jian)

For those who prefer a mountain sunrise over a lake sunrise, the Nine Creeks and Eighteen Gullies area in the southwestern hills is an excellent choice. This is a network of hiking trails that wind through bamboo forests, tea plantations, and small streams. It is less touristy than the lake area and offers a completely different atmosphere.

Why it is special: The sunrise here is not about a wide horizon but about light filtering through the bamboo. As the sun rises, the beams of light cut through the dense canopy, creating a dramatic, almost cathedral-like effect. The sound of running water and the rustling of bamboo leaves add to the sensory experience.

Best time to arrive: You will need to hike for about 30 to 40 minutes from the entrance to reach the best viewpoints. Start your hike at least one hour before sunrise. The trail is well-marked, but it can be slippery in the morning dew, so wear sturdy shoes.

Pro tip: The best spot is near the top of the trail, where there is a small pavilion called Longjing Pavilion. From there, you can see the tea terraces of Longjing Village in the distance. After the sunrise, you can walk down to Longjing Village and have breakfast at one of the local tea houses. The village is famous for its Longjing (Dragon Well) tea, and a cup of fresh tea at 7 a.m. is a reward worth waking up for.

5. The Qiantang River Bridge and the Six Harmonies Pagoda

The Qiantang River is famous for its tidal bore, but it also offers a spectacular sunrise view. The Six Harmonies Pagoda (Liuhe Ta) sits on a hill overlooking the river, and the combination of the pagoda, the bridge, and the wide river creates a grand, sweeping composition.

Why it is special: The sunrise here is more dramatic than at West Lake because the river is much wider and the sky seems larger. The pagoda itself is a masterpiece of ancient architecture, and the morning light illuminates its intricate brickwork. The nearby Qiantang River Bridge, built in the 1930s, adds a touch of industrial history to the natural beauty.

Best time to arrive: 5:30 a.m. in summer. The pagoda opens at 8 a.m., but you can walk around the grounds for free before that. The best photo spot is from the small park at the base of the hill, where you can frame the pagoda and the bridge together.

Pro tip: If you have a telephoto lens, you can capture the sunrise behind the bridge’s steel arches. Also, the area is less crowded than West Lake, so you can enjoy a peaceful morning. After the sunrise, you can visit the nearby Chinese Tea Museum, which opens at 9 a.m., to learn about Hangzhou’s tea culture.

6. The Top of Wushan Hill (Wushan or City God Hill)

Wushan Hill is located in the heart of Hangzhou, just south of West Lake. It is a low hill with several temples, pavilions, and a restored city wall. The top offers a 360-degree view of the city, the lake, and the river. It is a popular spot for locals to practice tai chi and fly kites in the morning.

Why it is special: The view from Wushan is unique because it combines the natural beauty of the lake with the urban skyline. You can see the modern skyscrapers of the city center rising behind the ancient pagodas of the lake. The sunrise here is more colorful because of the city lights reflecting off the morning mist.

Best time to arrive: The hill is open 24 hours, and the climb takes about 20 minutes. Aim to be at the top 40 minutes before sunrise. The best viewpoint is near the Chenghuang Pavilion (City God Pavilion), which is a two-story structure with an observation deck.

Pro tip: Bring a zoom lens to capture the details of the city waking up. You can also see the Qiantang River in the distance. After the sunrise, walk down the southern side of the hill to the Hefang Street area, which is a pedestrian shopping street. Most shops open at 9 a.m., but you can find a few early breakfast stalls serving soy milk and fried dough sticks.

Practical Tips for Sunrise Chasing in Hangzhou

What to Bring

  • A good flashlight or headlamp: Many trails are not well-lit before dawn.
  • Layered clothing: Mornings can be cool, even in summer, and the temperature drops significantly on the hills.
  • A tripod: If you are serious about photography, a tripod is essential for long exposures.
  • Water and snacks: Most sunrise spots are far from cafes, and you will be out for at least two hours.
  • A reusable bag: For your trash. The Chinese government has strict rules about littering, and you want to leave the spots as clean as you found them.

Transportation

  • By taxi or ride-hailing app: Didi is the most reliable app in China. Most sunrise spots are within a 15-minute drive from the city center. Expect to pay 20 to 40 RMB ($3 to $6) for a short trip.
  • By public bus: Buses start running at around 6 a.m., which is too late for sunrise. However, some night buses run until 11 p.m., so you can take one to the area the night before and wait.
  • By bike: Hangzhou has a public bike-sharing system called Hangzhou Public Bicycle. The bikes are sturdy and cheap, but you will need to download the app and register with a Chinese phone number. Riding to the lake at 5 a.m. is a magical experience, as the streets are empty.

Weather Considerations

  • Check the forecast: The best sunrises occur when there is a mix of low clouds and clear sky. Completely clear days can be less dramatic because there is no color in the sky.
  • Avoid foggy days: While mist is beautiful, thick fog can completely block the view. If the forecast says “heavy fog,” consider sleeping in.
  • Summer vs. winter: In summer (June to August), sunrise is around 5:00 a.m., and the weather is warm but humid. In winter (December to February), sunrise is around 6:45 a.m., and the temperature can drop to near freezing. Spring and autumn are the best seasons for comfortable temperatures and colorful skies.

Where to Eat Breakfast After the Sunrise

One of the best parts of an early morning adventure is the reward of a good breakfast. Hangzhou has a rich food culture, and the breakfast options are diverse.

1. Kui Yuan Guan (Kuiyuan Dumpling House)

Located near the southern end of West Lake, this small restaurant serves traditional Hangzhou-style dumplings (xiaolongbao) and wonton soup. It opens at 6:30 a.m., so it is perfect after a sunrise at Leifeng Pagoda or the Broken Bridge. The pork and crab roe dumplings are legendary.

Address: 205 Nanshan Road

2. Grandma’s Home (Waipo Jia)

This is a chain restaurant that serves home-style Hangzhou dishes, but it also offers a breakfast menu with congee, pickled vegetables, and fried bread. The branch near Wushan Hill opens at 7 a.m. and is a good option after a sunrise hike.

Address: 58 Wushan Road

3. Street Food Stalls

If you want a quick and cheap breakfast, look for street stalls selling jianbing (savory crepes), youtiao (fried dough sticks), and doujiang (soy milk). These stalls are common near the lake and in the residential areas. The quality is usually excellent, and the price is less than 10 RMB ($1.50).

Pro tip: Use the app Dianping (like Yelp) to find the highest-rated stalls near your location. The app is in Chinese, but you can use the translate function or ask a local for help.

Photography Tips for Sunrise at West Lake

Camera Settings

  • ISO: Keep it low (100-400) to avoid noise. Use a tripod to compensate for the slow shutter speed.
  • Aperture: Use f/8 to f/11 for a deep depth of field, ensuring both the foreground and background are sharp.
  • Shutter speed: Start with 1/30 second and adjust based on the brightness. For long exposures of the water, use 1 to 3 seconds.
  • White balance: Set it to “daylight” or “cloudy” to enhance the warm tones of the sunrise.

Composition

  • Use leading lines: The bridges, paths, and shorelines of West Lake create natural leading lines that draw the eye toward the sun.
  • Include silhouettes: The pagodas, willow trees, and boats make excellent silhouettes against the bright sky.
  • Reflect the sky: The still water of West Lake is perfect for reflection shots. Place the horizon line in the middle of the frame for a symmetrical composition.

Post-Processing

  • Increase contrast: Sunrise photos often look flat straight out of the camera. A slight increase in contrast will make the colors pop.
  • Adjust the temperature: If the photo looks too blue, warm it up by increasing the temperature slider.
  • Crop for impact: Remove any distracting elements from the edges of the frame to focus on the main subject.

The Cultural Significance of Sunrise in Hangzhou

Sunrise viewing is not just a tourist activity in Hangzhou; it is a deeply ingrained cultural practice. The Chinese concept of “zhao qi” (morning energy) is tied to the belief that the early morning hours are the most auspicious for health and spiritual practice. Many locals gather at West Lake before dawn to practice tai chi, qigong, or simply to walk and breathe the fresh air.

The sunrise also holds a special place in Chinese poetry and painting. The Tang dynasty poet Bai Juyi, who served as governor of Hangzhou, wrote extensively about the beauty of West Lake at dawn. His poem “On the Lake at Dawn” describes the “green hills and blue waters” that “seem to be painted.” The Song dynasty painter Ma Yuan famously depicted the “Misty Rain of West Lake” in his album leaves, capturing the ethereal quality of the morning light.

For the modern traveler, participating in this ancient tradition is a way to connect with the soul of the city. It is a reminder that despite the rapid modernization of China, there are still places where time seems to stand still.

Final Thoughts on Sunrise in Hangzhou

Hangzhou’s best places to see the sunrise are not just about the view; they are about the experience of being in a city that is both ancient and alive. Whether you choose the iconic pagodas, the serene lake, or the hidden mountain trails, you will find that the dawn in Hangzhou is a gift that keeps giving. The light changes every minute, the mist shifts, and the sounds of the city slowly wake up. It is a moment that belongs only to you and the few others who made the effort to be there.

So set your alarm, pack your camera, and step out into the dark. The sunrise over Hangzhou is waiting.
